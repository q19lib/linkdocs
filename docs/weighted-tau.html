    Comparing rankings

    Weighted tau

    Kendall's tau with each pair weighted by how high its items rank, so disagreements at the top count more than disagreements in the tail. Designed by Sebastiano Vigna for comparing web-scale rankings with many ties.

    Kendall’s tau counts every pair of items equally. For rankings, that is the wrong default: a search engine that swaps its first two results has made a more visible change than one that swaps positions 50,000,001 and 50,000,002. On a web-scale ranking, tail pairs outnumber top pairs by billions to one, so classical τ measures almost nothing but agreement in the tail. Sebastiano Vigna’s weighted τ (2015) fixes this by weighting each pair according to how high its items rank, while keeping τ’s treatment of ties and its O(nlog⁡n)O(n \log n)O(nlogn) computation.

    Definition

    Each pair (i,j)(i, j)(i,j) gets a weight wijw_{ij}wij​ that grows as the items rank higher. With sgn⁡\operatorname{sgn}sgn the sign function,

    τw=∑i<jwij sgn⁡(xi−xj) sgn⁡(yi−yj)∑i<jwij [xi≠xj]  ∑i<jwij [yi≠yj].\tau_w = \frac{\sum_{i<j} w_{ij}\, \operatorname{sgn}(x_i - x_j)\, \operatorname{sgn}(y_i - y_j)} {\sqrt{\sum_{i<j} w_{ij}\, [x_i \ne x_j]} \; \sqrt{\sum_{i<j} w_{ij}\, [y_i \ne y_j]}} .τw​=∑i<j​wij​[xi​=xj​]​∑i<j​wij​[yi​=yj​]​∑i<j​wij​sgn(xi​−xj​)sgn(yi​−yj​)​.

    With all weights equal to 1 this is exactly Kendall’s τ_b, including its handling of ties. Vigna’s recommended weight is additive hyperbolic: if rir_iri​ is the position of item iii (counting from 0) in a reference ranking,

    wij=1ri+1+1rj+1.w_{ij} = \frac{1}{r_i + 1} + \frac{1}{r_j + 1} .wij​=ri​+11​+rj​+11​.

    A pair involving the top item weighs at least 1; a pair of items around position one million weighs about 2×10−62 \times 10^{-6}2×10−6. Which ranking provides the positions? Using either xxx or yyy makes the measure asymmetric, so the standard choice computes τw\tau_wτw​ twice, once with positions from each ranking (ties broken by the other), and averages the two.

    Worked example

    Against PageRank on the running example:

    Compared with PageRank Kendall τ_b Weighted τ
    Harmonic centrality 0.975 0.972
    In-degree 0.866 0.915
    HITS authority 0.500 0.685
    Closeness 0.100 0.003

    In-degree and HITS authority agree with PageRank on the top node, C, and on most of the next few; their disagreements are lower down, so weighting raises their scores. Closeness puts D and F, two of PageRank’s bottom three, at its top, so weighting makes its agreement vanish.

    On real data

    For a random sample of 3,000 domains from the 2026-09-23 auction inventory that appear in Common Crawl’s graph, harmonic and PageRank positions have Kendall τ_b = 0.51 but weighted τ = 0.70. The two centralities agree considerably better on the best-ranked domains in the sample than on its long tail, where small value differences and ties shuffle positions. The top is what a buyer looks at first, and on the top the two Common Crawl rankings tell a more consistent story than classical τ suggests.

    Properties

    • Ties are handled as in τ_b. Pairs tied in one ranking still carry weight in the denominator, so a ranking that ties many top items is penalised.
    • The weight function is a choice. Hyperbolic weights fall off slowly, so the tail still contributes in aggregate. Steeper weights, such as 1/(r+1)21/(r+1)^21/(r+1)2, focus more sharply on the top. Vigna showed that any weighting that is a sum or product of per-item weights can be computed in the same time bound.
    • Fast. A weighted version of Knight’s merge-sort algorithm computes τw\tau_wτw​ in O(nlog⁡n)O(n \log n)O(nlogn) time, so comparing two rankings of 120 million domains takes seconds. SciPy’s scipy.stats.weightedtau implements the additive hyperbolic version by default.
    • Not a probability. Unlike τ, the value no longer translates into a share of agreeing pairs. Compare weighted τ values only with each other, computed with the same weights.

    When to use it

    Use weighted τ when comparing two full rankings where the top matters most and ties are common: two centralities on the same graph, two crawls’ PageRank, or a vendor score against an open one. When only the top kkk items are available, or the rankings cover different sets of items, Rank-biased overlap is the better tool. When every item matters equally, as in a statistical test of association, classical τ or Spearman’s rho remain appropriate.
