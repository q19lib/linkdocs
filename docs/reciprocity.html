    Network structure

    Reciprocity

    The share of links that are returned. In Common Crawl's 2026 domain graph about one link in eight has a link back, hundreds of thousands of times more than chance, from sites under common ownership, communities that cite each other and link exchanges.

    A link from uuu to vvv is reciprocated when vvv also links to uuu. Reciprocity is the share of a directed graph’s arcs that are reciprocated. It measures how symmetric a network is: friendship graphs are highly reciprocal, citation graphs barely at all, since a paper can cite only older papers. Link graphs sit in between, and the reciprocal part of a link graph has its own character: much of it comes from sites run by the same people and from deliberate link swaps.

    Definition

    With mmm arcs, the reciprocity is

    r=∣{(u,v)∈A:(v,u)∈A}∣m,r = \frac{\bigl|\{(u, v) \in A : (v, u) \in A\}\bigr|}{m} ,r=m​{(u,v)∈A:(v,u)∈A}​​,

    the fraction of arcs whose reverse also exists. Each mutual pair contributes two arcs.

    Where reciprocal links come from

    • Common ownership. A company’s sites in several countries, a publisher’s titles, or a brand’s shop, blog and support domains link to each other as a matter of course. The domain graph turns such links into mutual pairs (see page, host and domain graphs).
    • Communities. Sites in one field cite each other: research groups, local businesses, clubs of hobbyists.
    • Platform and infrastructure links. A site embeds a service, and the service’s pages link back to its customers’ sites in showcases or directories.
    • Link exchanges. “Link to me and I will link to you.” Google’s spam policies list excessive link exchanges as a link scheme (see link spam).

    Reciprocity matters for ranking because a mutual pair is a short cycle, and short cycles trap the random surfer: probability bounces between the two sites. Two sites exchanging links raise each other’s PageRank more than one-way links would, which is exactly why link exchanges became a manipulation tactic. Harmonic centrality is not fooled the same way: a returned link adds one in-link, at distance 1, and nothing more.

    Measuring it at scale

    Exact reciprocity needs, for every arc (u,v)(u, v)(u,v), a test of whether (v,u)(v, u)(v,u) exists: with both the graph and its transpose stored as sorted successor lists, one merged scan answers all tests in O(m)O(m)O(m). Without that, sampling works well. The estimate above put 2,000,000 sampled reversed arcs in a hash table and checked every arc of the graph against it in one pass; the sampling error for a share near 13% with that sample size is about 0.02 percentage points per standard deviation.

    What it means for a domain

    A handful of reciprocal links with closely related sites is normal. A link profile in which a large share of referring domains are also linked from the domain, especially unrelated ones, is the footprint of link exchanges. When evaluating a domain, compare its referring domains with the domains its old site linked to: heavy overlap with unrelated sites suggests swapped links, which search engines may discount (see link penalties).
