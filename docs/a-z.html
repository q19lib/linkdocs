    Count every walk that ends at a node, weighting a walk of length k by β to the power k. Short walks count most, everyone gets a baseline, and one parameter slides the measure from in-degree to eigenvector centrality.

    Page, host and domain graphs
    The same links give three different graphs depending on whether a node is a URL, a host name or a registered domain. Each level answers different questions and hides different problems.
    PageRank
    A node is important if important nodes link to it. PageRank makes that circular idea precise as the long-run behaviour of a random surfer who sometimes gets bored and jumps.
    PageRank as a linear system
    PageRank is also the solution of a sparse system of linear equations. That view brings faster solvers such as Gauss–Seidel, explains why the problem is well conditioned, and makes PageRank linear in the teleport vector.

    The bow-tie structure
    Seen from its largest strongly connected component, the web looks like a bow tie. Pages that reach the core sit on one side, pages the core reaches sit on the other, and stragglers hang off both.
    The Common Crawl web graph
    Common Crawl publishes free host- and domain-level link graphs every few months, with harmonic centrality and PageRank for every node. What the files contain, how they are built, and how to read them.
    The damping factor
    The probability α that PageRank's random surfer follows a link rather than jumping. It trades link structure against uniformity, sets the speed of convergence, and is conventionally 0.85 for no deep reason.
    The neighbourhood function
    For each distance t, how many pairs of nodes are within t links of each other. From it come the distance distribution, the average distance and the effective diameter, the robust ways to say how "small" a graph is.
    The Perron–Frobenius theorem
    The theorem that guarantees a nonnegative matrix has a dominant eigenvector with positive entries. It is why PageRank exists, why it is unique, and why eigenvector centrality fails on graphs that are not strongly connected.
    The random surfer
    The story behind PageRank. A surfer clicks random links, never presses Back, and now and then jumps to a random page; the share of time spent on each page is its PageRank.
    The web graph
    The directed graph with a node for every page, host or domain and an arc for every hyperlink between them.

    Majestic's two 0–100 scores. Citation Flow measures link volume and power; Trust Flow measures closeness in the link graph to manually reviewed trusted sites, a TrustRank-style idea. Their ratio is a popular spam heuristic.
    Trust propagation
    See TrustRank.
    TrustRank
    Pick a small set of sites a human has checked, and let trust flow outward from them along links, fading with each step. Spam sites, which reputable sites rarely link to, end up with little trust however many links they have.

    Weighted tau
    Kendall's tau with each pair weighted by how high its items rank, so disagreements at the top count more than disagreements in the tail. Designed by Sebastiano Vigna for comparing web-scale rankings with many ties.
