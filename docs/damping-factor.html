    PageRank in depth

    The damping factor

    The probability α that PageRank's random surfer follows a link rather than jumping. It trades link structure against uniformity, sets the speed of convergence, and is conventionally 0.85 for no deep reason.

    The damping factor α\alphaα is the one parameter of PageRank. At each step the random surfer follows a link with probability α\alphaα and teleports with probability 1−α1 - \alpha1−α. Brin and Page used α=0.85\alpha = 0.85α=0.85, and almost everyone since has copied them, including Common Crawl’s PageRank and the default of most graph libraries. The choice matters more than its conventional status suggests: it decides how far link influence travels, how fast the computation converges, and how stable the ranking is.

    What α controls

    The PageRank equation weights two terms:

    p=α pP+1−αn 1⊤.p = \alpha\, p P + \frac{1 - \alpha}{n}\, \mathbf{1}^\top .p=αpP+n1−α​1⊤.

    At α=0\alpha = 0α=0, links are ignored and every node scores 1/n1/n1/n. As α\alphaα grows, the link term takes over. Expanding the solution as a series makes the trade-off explicit:

    p=1−αn 1⊤∑k=0∞αkPk.p = \frac{1 - \alpha}{n}\, \mathbf{1}^\top \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \alpha^k P^k .p=n1−α​1⊤k=0∑∞​αkPk.

    PageRank sums the influence of paths of every length, with a path of length kkk weighted by αk\alpha^kαk. With α=0.85\alpha = 0.85α=0.85, a path of 5 links keeps 44% of its weight and a path of 20 links keeps 4%. With α=0.5\alpha = 0.5α=0.5, the same paths keep 3% and one in a million. Small α\alphaα makes PageRank local, close to a weighted in-degree; large α\alphaα makes it global.

    Convergence

    Power iteration reduces the error by a factor of about ∣λ2∣|\lambda_2|∣λ2​∣ per iteration, the second-largest eigenvalue modulus of the PageRank matrix. Haveliwala and Kamvar proved ∣λ2∣≤α|\lambda_2| \le \alpha∣λ2​∣≤α, with equality whenever the graph has at least two closed strongly connected components, which every web graph has. So to reach an error of ε\varepsilonε takes about

    k≈log⁡εlog⁡αk \approx \frac{\log \varepsilon}{\log \alpha}k≈logαlogε​

    iterations: 113 for ε=10−8\varepsilon = 10^{-8}ε=10−8 at α=0.85\alpha = 0.85α=0.85, but 1,833 at α=0.99\alpha = 0.99α=0.99. The running example converges faster than the bound because its two dangling nodes teleport every visitor, which shortens every walk. Higher α\alphaα is more faithful to the link structure and much more expensive.

    Sensitivity as α approaches 1

    As α→1\alpha \to 1α→1 the teleport vanishes, and PageRank approaches the stationary distribution of the raw walk, which puts all its mass in closed components such as the OUT side of the bow tie and dense spam clusters that link only to themselves. Boldi, Santini and Vigna studied PageRank as a function of α\alphaα and showed that the limit as α→1\alpha \to 1α→1 exists but concentrates on those closed components, the nodes “where the surfer gets stuck.” Rankings near α=1\alpha = 1α=1 are therefore dominated by rank sinks and change sharply with α\alphaα. Small α\alphaα has the opposite risk: the ranking barely uses the link structure at all.

    Why 0.85?

    The value is a compromise that Brin and Page reported without derivation. It keeps substantial link influence (mean run of 5.7 clicks between jumps), converges in about a hundred iterations, and keeps rank sinks in check. Several researchers have argued for other values. Avrachenkov, Litvak and Pham, analysing how mass splits between the giant component and the rest, suggested α=1/2\alpha = 1/2α=1/2. None of these displaced the convention: 0.85 remains the standard because everyone else uses it, which makes results comparable.

    Practical advice

    • Report α with every PageRank. A score computed with α=0.85\alpha = 0.85α=0.85 is not comparable with one computed at 0.5. The same holds for the dangling-node treatment and the teleport distribution (see dangling nodes and personalized PageRank).
    • Check stability. If a ranking you care about changes a lot between α=0.8\alpha = 0.8α=0.8 and 0.90.90.9, it rests on fragile structure, often a rank sink or a single strong link.
    • Do not chase precision you cannot use. The ranks and scores entry explains why the tenth digit of a PageRank value carries no information about a domain. Stopping at a relative change of 10−610^{-6}10−6 or 10−710^{-7}10−7 usually fixes every position that matters.
