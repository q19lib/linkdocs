    Comparing rankings

    Rank-biased overlap

    Compare two rankings by how much their top lists overlap at every depth, with geometrically decreasing weight on deeper levels. Works on truncated lists and on lists that do not contain the same items.

    Many rankings are only ever seen as top lists: the first ten search results, the hundred most central domains, the top thousand by a vendor’s score. Such lists are indefinite, cut off at an arbitrary depth, and usually non-conjoint: an item in one list may not appear in the other at all. Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho need both rankings over the same items and cannot cope. Rank-biased overlap (RBO), introduced by William Webber, Alistair Moffat and Justin Zobel in 2010, compares two lists by their overlap at each depth, weighting shallow depths more, and gives meaningful values even when only the tops are known.

    Overlap at a depth

    Let S1:dS_{1:d}S1:d​ and T1:dT_{1:d}T1:d​ be the first ddd items of two rankings. Their agreement at depth ddd is the fraction of items they share:

    Ad=∣S1:d∩T1:d∣d.A_d = \frac{|S_{1:d} \cap T_{1:d}|}{d} .Ad​=d∣S1:d​∩T1:d​∣​.

    Comparing top-kkk lists by AkA_kAk​ alone, or by their Jaccard similarity, is common and simple, but it ignores order within the top kkk and depends heavily on the arbitrary choice of kkk.

    Definition

    RBO averages the agreements at all depths with geometric weights:

    RBO(S,T,p)=(1−p)∑d=1∞pd−1Ad.\text{RBO}(S, T, p) = (1 - p) \sum_{d=1}^{\infty} p^{d-1} A_d .RBO(S,T,p)=(1−p)d=1∑∞​pd−1Ad​.

    The persistence p∈(0,1)p \in (0, 1)p∈(0,1) models a reader who looks at item d+1d + 1d+1 with probability ppp after seeing item ddd. Small ppp makes RBO focus sharply on the top; ppp close to 1 spreads attention deeper. Webber and colleagues computed that with p=0.9p = 0.9p=0.9 the first ten depths carry about 86% of the total weight. RBO is 1 for identical infinite rankings and 0 for rankings that share no items at any depth.

    Truncated lists

    Real lists stop at some depth kkk, so the sum cannot be evaluated to infinity. RBO comes with bounds:

    • RBO_min assumes the lists share nothing beyond depth kkk: a lower bound.
    • RBO_res, the residual, is the most that the unseen depths could add.
    • RBO_ext extrapolates by assuming that agreement stays at AkA_kAk​ beyond depth kkk, a point estimate inside the bounds and the value usually reported.

    Because the weights decay geometrically, the residual shrinks fast as kkk grows; for p=0.9p = 0.9p=0.9 and k=30k = 30k=30, the uncertainty is a few hundredths.

    2 facebook.com google.com
    3 google.com facebook.com
    4 instagram.com googletagmanager.com
    5 googletagmanager.com instagram.com
    6 youtube.com cloudflare.com
    7 gstatic.com gstatic.com

    10 gmpg.org gmpg.org

    The agreement starts at 1 (both put googleapis.com first), dips to 0.5 at depth 2, returns to 1 at depths 3 and 5, and settles near 0.75 to 0.8 from depth 10 to depth 30. Over the top 30 lists, RBO_ext is 0.81 with p=0.9p = 0.9p=0.9 and 0.85 with p=0.5p = 0.5p=0.5: the two centralities largely agree on the very top of the web, and diverge more as the lists get longer, as the PageRank entry’s examples of PageRank-only domains show.

    Properties

    • Order within the top matters. Swapping positions 1 and 2 lowers A1A_1A1​ to 0 if neither list has the other’s first item at depth 1, and the deep terms barely notice.
    • No common universe needed. Items that appear in only one list simply never count towards overlap, so a vendor’s top list can be compared with Common Crawl’s even if neither covers the other’s domains.
    • Ties need a convention. RBO assumes strict orders; Webber and colleagues describe how to treat tied items as sharing their depths.
    • Not a correlation. RBO is a similarity between 0 and 1, not a coefficient between −1 and 1, and a value of 0.8 does not mean the same thing as τ=0.8\tau = 0.8τ=0.8.

    When to use it

    Use RBO for search results, top-kkk lists and leaderboards, especially when the lists come from different sources with different coverage: two link indexes, two crawls, or a vendor’s “top domains” against an open ranking. Use weighted tau when both full rankings over the same items are available and ties are common; use plain overlap at a fixed kkk only when the reader really does stop at exactly kkk.
